# Gold Layer Documentation: Customer Segmentation Case Study

* **Project:** Customer Segmentation Case Study
* **Database Schema:** `dunnhumby_project.gold`
* **Data Architecture:** Databricks Medallion Architecture (Silver to Gold)

---

## Overview

The Gold layer contains refined, business-ready datasets built for executive reporting, Business Intelligence (BI) dashboards, and predictive modeling. It transforms raw, transaction-level data from the Silver layer into clear, high-level metrics that answer core commercial questions about customer loyalty, revenue concentration, and product performance.

---

## Table 1: `rfm_segments`

**Business Purpose:** Categorizes every household into a distinct behavioral group based on how recently they shopped, how often they visit, and how much they spend (Recency, Frequency, and Monetary value). This is the primary table used to tailor marketing campaigns and retention strategies.

**Data Structure:** One row per unique household (`household_key`).

| Column Name | Data Type | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `household_key` | INT | Primary Key. Unique identifier for each household. |
| `segment` | STRING | Assigned behavioral group (e.g., Champions, Loyal, At Risk). |
| `last_day` | INT | The most recent day (on a 1–711 timeline) the household made a purchase. |
| `frequency` | INT | Total number of unique shopping trips completed by the household. |
| `monetary` | DECIMAL | Total lifetime spend ($) by the household. |

### Scoring & Segmentation Logic

Households are scored on a scale of 1 to 5 (with 5 being the highest) by dividing the customer base into five equal tiers:

* **Recency Score (R):** Ranks customers by `last_day` (higher scores indicate more recent visits).
* **Monetary Score (M):** Ranks customers by `monetary` spend (higher scores indicate higher total spend).

Based on these scores, customers are grouped into five actionable segments:

1. **Champions:** Average of R and M scores is 4 or higher (recent, high-spending shoppers).
2. **Loyal:** Average of R and M scores is 3 or higher (consistent, reliable shoppers).
3. **New / Promising:** High Recency (4 or 5) with Low Monetary spend (1 or 2).
4. **At Risk (High Value):** Low Recency (1 or 2) with High Monetary spend (4 or 5) — valuable past customers who need re-engagement.
5. **Occasional:** All remaining households.

## Table 2: `customer_quintiles`

**Business Purpose:** Ranks all households by total lifetime spend and divides them into five equal-sized groups (quintiles) to measure revenue concentration across the customer base.

**Data Structure:** One row per spend tier (`quintile`, numbered 1 through 5).

| Column Name | Data Type | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `quintile` | INT | Spend tier (`1` = Top 20% highest spenders; `5` = Bottom 20% spenders). |
| `customers` | INT | Total number of households within the tier. |
| `revenue` | DECIMAL | Total revenue ($) generated by the tier. |
| `pct_revenue` | DECIMAL | Share of total company revenue (%) driven by the tier. |

> **Key Business Finding:** This table validates the Pareto Principle (the 80/20 rule) within the retail dataset, showing that the top 20% of customers (Quintile 1) generate over 53% of total company revenue.

## Table 3: `department_sales`

**Business Purpose:** Summarizes total sales and overall revenue share across individual store departments to highlight core revenue drivers.

**Data Structure:** One row per retail department (`department`).

| Column Name | Data Type | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `department` | STRING | Name of the retail department (e.g., `GROCERY`, `DRUG GM`, `PRODUCE`). |
| `total_sales` | DECIMAL | Total revenue ($) generated by the department. |
| `percentage` | DECIMAL | The department's share of total company revenue (%). |

> **Data Quality Standard:** Non-merchandise categories (`KIOSK-GAS`, `MISC SALES TRAN`, and `COUPON/MISC`) are excluded from this table so that financial metrics accurately reflect physical product sales.
